Educational decision support

Rehab Candidate Review

A structured questionnaire for identifying strengths, concerns, and missing information in a possible inpatient rehabilitation referral.

Before beginning

This prototype does not make admission, denial, coverage, or clinical-care decisions.

  • Do not enter names, dates of birth, medical-record numbers, or room numbers.
  • Do not paste chart notes or other identifying information.
  • Use a generic label such as Patient 1.
  • All answers remain in this browser session and are not saved by the app.

Use only a non-identifying label.

Section 1

Medical readiness

Identify conditions that could make transfer unsafe or premature.

Is the patient medically stable for transfer?
Is a major procedure, surgery, or acute diagnostic workup still pending?
Are vital signs, pain, acute symptoms, and medical conditions reasonably stable or manageable?
Is the patient currently receiving a continuous IV infusion?
What is the patient’s current oxygen requirement?
Does oxygen saturation significantly decline with exertion?
Does the patient have a documented ejection fraction below 35%?

Section 2

Prior function and current decline

Determine whether a meaningful new rehabilitation problem exists.

What was the patient’s general level of function before the current illness or injury?
How significant is the new functional decline?
Which current functional areas are affected?

Section 3

Therapy requirements

Identify the intensity and combination of rehabilitation services needed.

Which therapy disciplines appear necessary?
Are clear and measurable functional goals documented?
Could the patient’s rehabilitation needs likely be met at a lower intensity of care?

Section 4

Participation and therapy tolerance

Evaluate whether the patient can meaningfully engage and benefit.

What is the patient’s current ability to participate in therapy?
Can the patient participate in approximately three total hours of therapy per day, five days per week, in separated sessions?

Therapy may be divided throughout the day and is not performed as one continuous three-hour session.

Can the patient follow instructions, learn strategies, or participate with cueing and supervision?

Section 5

Rehabilitation potential

Determine whether measurable practical improvement is reasonably expected.

Is meaningful functional improvement reasonably expected during an inpatient rehabilitation stay?
Does the patient currently require maximum assistance for mobility or activities of daily living?
How would you describe motivation and engagement?

Section 6

Active medical-management needs

Identify medical issues that directly affect rehabilitation and may require close oversight.

Which active medical issues affect rehabilitation?
How is the patient’s diabetes currently managed?
If the WBC count is above 14, is the cause known and being treated or monitored?
Is kidney function manageable at the rehabilitation level of care?
Is the patient receiving active chemotherapy or radiation?

This reflects a facility-specific operational requirement. Any chemotherapy or radiation pause must be coordinated with the oncology team and is not a general medical recommendation.

Does the patient appear to require frequent physician oversight specifically related to rehabilitation?
Could these medical issues likely be managed safely at a lower level of care?

Section 7

Discharge feasibility

Consider whether rehabilitation can realistically support a safer discharge.

Is there a likely discharge destination?
Is caregiver or environmental support available or reasonably obtainable?
Does confusion, impaired memory, or reduced safety awareness create a need for caregiver support after discharge?

Section 8

Documentation completeness

Identify information that should be obtained before a final clinical review.

Which important information is missing or unclear?
